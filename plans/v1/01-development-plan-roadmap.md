## How the plans depend on each other

- Start with infrastructure and operating rules: timetable advice needs a clear account of where trains can move, wait, and pass.
- Develop services/timetabling and fleet/depot plans together so scheduled departures can be assigned to feasible train duties.
- Build passenger planning around actual timetable journeys, then coordinate fare and cost decisions with the economy plan.
- Work on architecture and player workflows throughout these discussions. Settle shared interfaces, save requirements, and interaction constraints before coding the related features.
- Use the first-playable plan to choose a bounded implementation slice across these systems rather than completing every subsystem in isolation.

## Common checklist for every future plan

Each future document should cover:

1. **Goals:** the player capability, intended outcome, and success criteria.
2. **Confirmed decisions:** requirements inherited from the vision and choices explicitly settled during planning.
3. **Open questions:** unresolved alternatives and the decisions needed before implementation.
4. **Player workflow:** what the player sees, edits, confirms, and learns from feedback.
5. **Simulation rules:** relevant behaviour, constraints, edge cases, and failure handling.
6. **Minimum data and interface changes:** persisted state, derived state, and subsystem contracts needed for the feature; avoid speculative schemas.
7. **Dependencies:** required upstream behaviour and decisions shared with other plans.
8. **Acceptance scenarios:** concrete examples demonstrating correct behaviour, meaningful failure cases, and applicable performance requirements.
9. **Deferred features:** explicitly excluded work and future possibilities that must not silently expand v1.

A detailed plan is ready for implementation when its blocking questions are resolved and its acceptance scenarios are testable. Keep remaining non-blocking ideas explicitly deferred.

## Boundaries and next discussion

These documents do not authorize application changes or settle numerical cost, demand, or movement rules. Do not infer manual signalling controls or automatic timetable generation from operational realism or optional assistance.

Take up **interface and player experience** next. It is the last subsystem plan outstanding, and it now carries a backlog from every other document: simple construction controls and affected-service previews from section 02; the timetable editor, time-distance diagram, map route selection and assistance review from section 03; the catalogue browser, fleet and depot panels and duty assignment from section 04; the three demand layers, load overlays and the traveller inspector from section 05; cost previews, the weekly statement and the contract browser from section 06; and from section 08 the presentation of pending validation, effective simulation rate, undo, and save failures.

Section 09 then bounds the first playable slice across all of them, and should be taken last because it selects from what the other plans have settled rather than deciding anything new.

Four smaller pieces of work are worth closing whenever convenient, all narrow enough to settle without a full planning discussion: verifying and balancing section 04's catalogue figures; writing section 02's separation formulae against those braking rates; calibrating section 05's coefficients; and sourcing section 06's cost figures from Swedish evidence. Section 08's performance targets cannot be validated by discussion at all — they need an implementation to measure against.

Revisit the [game vision](00-game-vision.md) when a confirmed decision changes the overall direction. It now records the settled mechanics from sections 02 to 06 and 08, and marks the draft/published timetable, shared prediction, and destination attraction proposals as resolved.
